THE AUTHOR

Rev. Olumayowa Alaba Akinbade

Methodist clergyman, writer and advocate for ethical and transformative leadership.

Rev. Olumayowa Alaba Akinbade is a Methodist clergyman whose ministry, academic interests and writing explore the relationship between faith, ethics, leadership and public life. His work is shaped by a conviction that moral integrity should inform not only personal conduct but also the decisions, practices and cultures of the institutions people serve.

He holds a Bachelor of Theology from Immanuel College of Theology and Christian Education, Ibadan, and a Bachelor of Technology in Crop, Soil and Pest Management from the Federal University of Technology, Akure. He is pursuing postgraduate theological studies at Wesley University, Ondo, specialising in Ethics.

Areas of interest

His interests include Christian ethical leadership, institutional integrity, public theology, interreligious coexistence and social transformation. He is particularly concerned with the ethical challenges that arise when religious conviction, institutional loyalty and moral responsibility come into tension. His work seeks to encourage careful reflection on how individuals and institutions can respond to such challenges with honesty, courage and accountability.

He is also interested in the contribution of Christian ethics to peaceful coexistence in religiously diverse societies. For him, meaningful engagement between faith and society requires both conviction and a willingness to recognise the dignity of others.

Writing and AETL

Through Advocacy for Ethical & Transformative Leadership (AETL), he seeks to create space for thoughtful public conversation about leadership, faith, governance, institutional culture and social responsibility. The platform reflects his interest in bringing ethical questions into closer conversation with the realities of the Church and wider society.

His opinion papers examine contemporary issues, question assumptions and invite readers to consider the moral implications of individual choices and institutional practices. Rather than treating ethics as an abstract subject, his writing seeks to connect reflection with responsible action and constructive change.

AETL is an independent editorial initiative, and its Opinion Papers are intended to encourage informed reflection and public engagement. They are not presented as peer-reviewed academic publications.
